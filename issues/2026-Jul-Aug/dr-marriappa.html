Dr. Marriappa

Professor and Head
Department of Anatomy
Madras Veterinary College

A Formidable First Impression

It is indeed a great opportunity for me to express my memories of my chief, teacher, examiner, research guide and mentor, the late Dr. Marriappa.

I vividly remember my very first class in Anatomy. On the dais stood a tall, well-dressed and imposing figure with a stern expression. At the outset, Dr. Marriappa gave us strict instructions regarding etiquette, punctuality, the wearing of an apron and the prescribed dress code.

Our seniors had already created a certain amount of fear in our minds. They cautioned us that Anatomy was a difficult subject and that examinations could be particularly demanding when Dr. Marriappa was the examiner.

From Fear to Fascination

To overcome this fear and strengthen our understanding of the subject, my classmate Vijayalakshmi and I began spending our leisure hours in the Department of Anatomy, studying osteology.

It was during these visits that we often found Dr. Marriappa engaged in the dissection of elephant foetuses. Occasionally, he would call us over and carefully show us the dissected anatomical structures.

Those encounters gradually instilled in us a spirit of enquiry and a desire to learn more. The fear and inhibitions with which we had originally approached Anatomy slowly disappeared and were replaced by curiosity and fascination.

A Dedicated Teacher and Anatomist

Despite being Professor and Head of the Department, and at times carrying additional administrative responsibilities as Associate Dean, Dr. Marriappa regularly delivered lectures and conducted practical classes.

His teaching reflected remarkable devotion to the subject and an immense depth of authenticated knowledge. Anatomy, a subject often regarded by students as difficult and demanding, became interesting and enjoyable because of his dedication and mastery of teaching.

Through the quintessence of his teaching, Dr. Marriappa had the ability to transform a complex subject into something simpler, meaningful and delightful. His teaching created in me a lasting passion for Anatomy.

Contributions to Comparative Anatomy

Dr. Marriappa made important contributions to comparative anatomy and histology. His research encompassed mammalian anatomical structures, with particular attention to the Indian elephant (Elephas maximus) and the Asiatic water buffalo.

His detailed anatomical investigations reflected his extraordinary capacity for observation, meticulous dissection and documentation.

Dr. Marriappa wrote a book, Anatomy of Elephants.

From Student to Colleague

Subsequently, I joined the Department of Anatomy in 1963 as an Assistant Lecturer.

Only after joining the Department did I fully understand the man behind the apparently stern exterior. Although Dr. Marriappa could initially appear reserved and uncompromising, I came to realise that he was, in reality, a very compassionate person whose professional life was governed by a strong sense of discipline.

He was a fountainhead of knowledge and remained consistently committed to progressive learning. From him I learnt not only Anatomy, but also the art of teaching and the importance of maintaining discipline in academic life.

Postgraduate Research under Dr. Marriappa

When I opted for postgraduate studies in Anatomy, Dr. Marriappa entrusted me with a topic that had not been explored by my predecessors: “Histology and Histochemistry of the Pineal Gland.”

I later learnt that he had told other members of the staff that Lalitha alone could do justice to the topic. His confidence in me was both a responsibility and a source of encouragement.

Under his guidance, I successfully completed my thesis to his satisfaction. I remain deeply grateful to him for choosing me to undertake research on the pineal gland.

A Mentor's Lifelong Influence

The knowledge I gained through that research subsequently became an important foundation for my continuing intellectual and professional interests.

The profound knowledge of Anatomy that I acquired from my friend, philosopher and guide contributed immensely to my later professional life.

Dr. Marriappa was an epitome of Veterinary Anatomy and a magnificent guide. His scholarship, discipline, dedication to teaching and concern for his students left an indelible impression on those fortunate enough to learn under him.

Guru Vandanam

I remain forever indebted to my fatherly figure and Guru, Dr. Marriappa.

His lessons extended far beyond the confines of the Anatomy classroom. They taught us discipline, perseverance, intellectual curiosity and devotion to one's profession.

I remember him with profound gratitude and reverence, and continue to cherish his guidance with his eternal blessings.

Reminiscence by Dr. P. S. Lalitha.
