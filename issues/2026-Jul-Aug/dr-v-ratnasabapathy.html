Reminiscence of Dedicated Stalwarts

Remembering those who served with distinction, inspired generations and enriched our profession.

Area of Contribution
Animal Genetics, Veterinary Education and Research

Dr. V. Ratnasabapathy

B.V.Sc., Ph.D.

Former Director of Veterinary Education and Research

An Astute Scientist and Administrator

My admiration goes back to this astute scientist, Dr. V. Ratnasabapathy, affectionately referred to as Dr. VR, who served as Director of Veterinary Education and Research during the early 1970s.

He was a veterinarian who firmly believed that the veterinary profession should retain its professional identity and should not be placed under the control of another profession or a non-professional administrator.

One of his great strengths was his recognition and encouragement of researchers in the animal sciences.

Reinvigorating Veterinary Research

Dr. VR revitalised the Research Council and the Scientific Workers Conference following the separation of Madras Veterinary College from the State Animal Husbandry Department.

Researchers were expected to work hard and present their findings before their peers. Research programmes were subjected to critical review and presentation by the lead scientists.

I regarded this system of rigorous review as an excellent principle in research management and later followed the same approach during my own short tenure as Director of Research under Dr. Kadirvel as Vice-Chancellor.

A Rebound Period in Research Management

I would describe Dr. VR's tenure as a rebound period in research management.

The Livestock Research Station, Kattupakkam, and the Sheep Breeding Research Station, Sandynallah were among the research institutions that particularly attracted his attention.

A pioneer in animal genetics, he strongly advocated professional leadership within the academic departments of Madras Veterinary College.

Scientist, Policymaker and Administrator

Dr. Ratnasabapathy was regarded as a scientist who combined knowledge and technology with policymaking and effective administration.

Discipline was an important feature of his administration. He personally observed the arrival of teachers on the college campus, at times standing in front of the Dean's chamber in the Red Building.

He was also known for his strong power of argument. When Government Orders were issued without adequate consultation or without taking into account his professional proposals, he was prepared to argue firmly against their implementation.

Encouragement at a Formative Stage

Rewinding my own memory lane, I remember being greatly appreciated by Dr. VR during one of the hostel day celebrations in 1972, when Dr. Michael, the regular Warden, was on leave.

From that time onwards, he continued to encourage me professionally.

He nominated me for a programme in Japan, deputed me to Aavin where I served as a dairy bacteriologist, and much later included me in the TANUVAS Vice-Chancellor panel of 2002.

These gestures reflected his ability to recognise individuals and encourage their professional development.

The Challenge of Institutional Reorganisation

A difficult period in Dr. VR's career came when the Directorate of Veterinary Education and Research was merged with Tamil Nadu Agricultural University.

He strongly desired that veterinary education and research should retain their independent professional identity. His efforts to remain outside the unified agricultural university structure, however, did not succeed under the prevailing national policy of bringing the agricultural sciences under a common institutional umbrella.

Maintaining Professional Dignity

Until the date of his superannuation, Dr. Ratnasabapathy continued to maintain his professional dignity and administrative independence.

His career represented a distinctive combination of scientific knowledge, research leadership, professional conviction and administrative strength.

A Scientist to be Remembered

To me, Dr. V. Ratnasabapathy remains a stand-alone scientist whose contributions to animal genetics, veterinary education, research management and professional administration deserve enduring recognition.

He should be remembered forever by the veterinary profession for the principles he upheld and the institutions and scientists he encouraged.

Reminiscence by Dr. K. S. Palaniswami, General Secretary, TTPA.
