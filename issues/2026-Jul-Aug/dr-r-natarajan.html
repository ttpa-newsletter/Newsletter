Reminiscence of Dedicated Stalwarts

Remembering those who served with distinction, inspired generations and enriched our profession.

Profile at a Glance

Qualification
B.Sc., B.V.Sc., M.Sc.

Position
Retired Professor and Head,
Department of Veterinary Pharmacology and Toxicology,
Madras Veterinary College

Area of Contribution
Veterinary Pharmacology and Toxicology

Dr. R. Natarajan

B.Sc., B.V.Sc., M.Sc.

Retired Professor and Head
Department of Veterinary Pharmacology and Toxicology
Madras Veterinary College

Early Life and Veterinary Education

Dr. R. Natarajan was born on 10 November 1929.

After completing his B.Sc. in Chemistry from Pachaiyappa's College, he joined the B.V.Sc. degree programme at Madras Veterinary College in 1950.

He completed his B.V.Sc. degree in 1954 and subsequently served as a Veterinary Assistant Surgeon in North Arcot District.

Entry into Postgraduate Pharmacology

Dr. Natarajan applied for postgraduate study at Madras Veterinary College under the Government of India Research Training Scholarship Scheme.

He was selected for postgraduate study in both Physiology and Pharmacology.

At that time, the Professor of Physiology already had a research scholar in the Department. Dr. C. K. Velayudhan Nair, who was heading the Department of Veterinary Pharmacology, therefore accepted him as his research scholar.

Dr. Natarajan joined the M.Sc. programme in 1957 and completed it in 1960.

Beginning of an Academic Career

After completing his postgraduate degree, Dr. Natarajan joined the Department of Veterinary Pharmacology as an Additional Assistant Lecturer.

He subsequently held several academic positions in the Department and eventually became Professor and Head in 1979.

Research on Gloriosa superba

During his postgraduate research, Dr. Natarajan studied the various pharmacological effects of Gloriosa superba.

His background in Chemistry proved particularly useful in preparing different plant extracts required for his experimental studies.

This combination of chemistry and pharmacology contributed significantly to his experimental research approach.

Specialised Training in France

Dr. Natarajan was awarded a French Government Scholarship for specialised postgraduate training in Physiology and Pharmacology.

He underwent this training at École Nationale Vétérinaire, France, during 1964–1965.

Innovations in Experimental Pharmacology

Dr. Natarajan had a strong interest in experimental pharmacology and contributed several practical innovations to laboratory teaching and research.

Among his notable contributions were:

  • A device for recording intestinal motility using a Liebig's condenser.
  • A device for an isolated heart perfusion apparatus.
  • Lightweight recording levers fabricated using plastic tongue cleaners for recording delicate movements of isolated tissues.

These innovations reflected his ability to adapt readily available materials for experimental pharmacology at a time when specialised laboratory equipment was limited.

An Inspiring Teacher and Research Guide

Dr. Natarajan's classes inspired many students to choose Pharmacology for their master's degree.

He guided many postgraduate students in their research work and helped strengthen the experimental tradition of the Department.

His interest in experimental models and practical pharmacology left a lasting influence on his students.

Dr. R. Natarajan Award

As a mark of respect for their teacher, some of Dr. Natarajan's former students jointly instituted an award in the Indian Society of Veterinary Pharmacology and Toxicology (ISVPT) in 2012.

The award, named the “Dr. R. Natarajan Award”, is presented to a research scholar selecting a novel experimental model relevant to Pharmacology or Toxicology.

The award is presented during the annual ISVPT conference.

Service to the Indian Veterinary Journal

Dr. Natarajan retired from service in 1987.

Following retirement, he continued to contribute to the veterinary profession by serving as an Associate Editor of the Indian Veterinary Journal for approximately a decade.

Personality and Personal Interests

Dr. Natarajan was remembered as a highly disciplined and soft-spoken person.

He had a keen interest in photography.

He also took the unusual and valuable step of handwriting his autobiography, preserving a personal record of his life and career.

An Enduring Legacy

Dr. R. Natarajan made lasting contributions to Veterinary Pharmacology and Toxicology through his teaching, experimental innovations, postgraduate research guidance and professional service.

His work helped inspire a generation of students to pursue advanced study and research in Pharmacology.

After a brief period of illness, Dr. Natarajan passed away on 9 February 2012.

His memory continues through his students, his academic contributions and the Dr. R. Natarajan Award instituted in his honour.

Reminiscence compiled by Dr. P. Sriram, Secretary, TTPA.
