Leadership at Madras Veterinary College

Dr. Bertie A. D'Souza served as Principal of Madras Veterinary College and also acted as Director of Animal Husbandry during the late 1960s.

He was remembered as an able academic administrator who contributed significantly to strengthening the relationship between Madras Veterinary College and the Department of Animal Husbandry.

Veterinary Service During the Rinderpest Era

Dr. D'Souza played an important role in coordinating the activities of Madras Veterinary College and the Department of Animal Husbandry during a period when rinderpest posed a major threat to livestock.

Veterinary preventive medicine assumed particular importance during this period, and his academic and administrative leadership helped strengthen the connection between veterinary education and field animal-health services.

The Golden Jubilee of Madras Veterinary College

An important event associated with his period of service was the Golden Jubilee celebration of Madras Veterinary College.

As recorded in this reminiscence, Prime Minister Shri Jawaharlal Nehru inaugurated a scientific exhibition on the college campus on 17 January 1955 as part of the Golden Jubilee celebrations.

The occasion remains an important landmark in the institutional memory of Madras Veterinary College.

A Preventive Medicine Specialist

Dr. D'Souza was acclaimed for his knowledge and professional acumen in Veterinary Preventive Medicine.

He had a particular interest in the prevention of zoonotic diseases, including diseases transmitted from dogs to human beings.

His work reflected the important relationship between veterinary medicine, animal health and public health.

Strengthening Clinical Activities

As Professor of Preventive Medicine, Dr. D'Souza gave considerable importance to clinical activities.

Under his guidance, clinical training received substantial emphasis, helping students relate classroom knowledge to practical disease prevention and animal-health management.

A Memorable Teacher

Dr. D'Souza was remembered as a strict disciplinarian, but behind that disciplined exterior was a kind-hearted personality.

His teaching ability was particularly evident when he delivered lectures to final-year students at the erstwhile Dobbin Hall.

His combination of professional knowledge, discipline and clarity of presentation made a lasting impression on his students.

Contribution to Academic Publishing

Dr. D'Souza served as President of the Editorial Board of the Madras Veterinary College Annual during 1954–1955.

His academic interests extended to reviewing professional literature.

He reviewed the book Diseases of Feedlot Cattle by Rue Jensen and commented on its usefulness to veterinarians.

Indo-American Veterinary Cooperation

Among his broader professional contributions was his association with the Indo-American Team for Veterinary Science and Animal Husbandry.

Such international collaborations contributed to the exchange of scientific knowledge and the development of veterinary education and animal husbandry during an important period of institutional growth in India.

An Enduring Institutional Memory

Dr. Bertie A. D'Souza is remembered for his contributions as a teacher of Veterinary Preventive Medicine, academic administrator and Principal of Madras Veterinary College.

His commitment to discipline, preventive medicine, clinical education and institutional development earned him a distinctive place in the history of Madras Veterinary College.

Following his retirement, he settled in Australia.
